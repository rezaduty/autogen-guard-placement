Multi-agent frameworks give a defender several places to put a guard. We hold one prompt-injection guard fixed and move it between the three interception points AutoGen exposes: the model client, the runtime message bus and the tool boundary. On 150 probes built from real documents, with benign passages matched on length and hard negatives that isolate single surface features, the unit of text a placement hands the guard changes its verdict for every guard tested. A DeBERTa classifier flags 8 of 50 attacks inside the document and 45 as the passage alone, a language-model guard on the agents' model the reverse, 50 and 29, and a larger language-model guard keeps full recall but flags 15 benign documents against 0 benign passages. AutoGen's own memory formatting lowers the agents'-size guard from 50 to 33 of 50 (p = 1.5e-5). In team runs on 10 test carriers, blocking prevents every observed breach where the guard receives the text in time, except memory at the model client. A regular-expression denylist in front of the team blocks 74.0% of attacks and 76.0% of benign documents, because it decides on the surrounding document. Beyond injected instructions, AutoGen's termination check reads data: a document that contains the termination keyword ends the team in every run, a model-client guard prevents none of these stops, and a bus or tool guard prevents all. LangGraph and CrewAI show the same timing, and CrewAI's model hook flags the framework's own prompt in 10 of 10 benign runs. Every number is re-derived by an independent verifier.
